1. The climate signal

The 2026 Pacific Dataviz Challenge requires at least one dataset from its official climate-change dataset list. This dashboard uses the official Blue Pacific 2050 indicator “Number of people affected by disaster” as the featured climate-and-disaster indicator and connects it to TWICT's digital resilience work.

Official indicator: Number of people affected by disaster

Official Pacific Data Hub / Blue Pacific 2050 climate-and-disasters data: Tonga is shown at approximately **80,000 people affected by disasters** in the challenge dataset covering 2020–2023. This value is used here as the Tonga country-level figure; retain the source attribution and update if the official PDH.stat extract is revised.

Why this matters for digital resilience

  • Disasters can interrupt access to communication and services.
  • Women, children and vulnerable groups can face additional barriers.
  • Digital literacy helps people identify reliable information and avoid scams.
  • Cyber safety is essential when communities rely more heavily on digital channels.
  • STEM and data skills help young people understand risk and design solutions.

2. What TWICT does

TWICT's mission is to empower women and girls in Tonga through ICT, STEAM and technical fields while advancing participation, equality and digital confidence.

Programme ecosystem

Impact pathway

Access → digital skills → confidence → safer online participation → community resilience → greater ability to respond, recover and innovate.

Priority programme areas include Digital Literacy, Cyber Safety, STEM, Digital Advocacy, Digital Self-Defense, Internet Governance, Community-Centered Connectivity, Sustainable Peering Infrastructure, GEDSI and climate resilience.

3. TWICT programme areas

Digital Literacy

Practical skills for communities, women, youth and schools to participate confidently in the digital economy.

Cyber Safety

Awareness and practical protection against cyberbullying, scams, online abuse and other digital risks.

STEM

School outreach, science activities and opportunities that encourage girls and young people into STEM pathways.

Digital Advocacy

Using technology and evidence to support public engagement, policy dialogue and inclusive decision-making.

Climate & Digital Resilience

Connecting climate awareness with communication, data, digital safety and community preparedness.

Connectivity

Exploring community-centred connectivity and sustainable digital infrastructure for underserved communities.

Women in Tech

Creating pathways for women and girls to learn, lead, mentor and participate in ICT.

GEDSI

Embedding gender equality, disability inclusion and social inclusion across programmes and engagement.

4. From data to action

Resilience framework

What success looks like

  • More women and girls confidently use digital technologies.
  • Students see STEM as a pathway for solving local problems.
  • Communities recognise and respond to digital threats.
  • People can access trusted information during climate and disaster events.
  • Local evidence informs policy dialogue and preparedness.
  • Digital inclusion supports recovery and continuity of essential activities.
